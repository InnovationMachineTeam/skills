# Synthesis and Quality

## Cluster candidates

Cluster by intended outcome, preconditions, effects, and validation—not surface wording. Preserve provenance for every member. Mark copied or derived lineages so frequency does not masquerade as independent evidence.

## Handle contradictions

Do not average incompatible rules. Create variants and identify the dimension that explains the difference: host, risk tier, reversibility, user authority, data sensitivity, runtime, or organizational policy. If the distinction is unknown, retain an unresolved contradiction.

## Score usefulness

Assess:

- evidence fidelity;
- recurrence and source independence;
- portability and explicit assumptions;
- operational usefulness;
- safety and authority clarity;
- rights and attribution status;
- testability and expected maintenance cost.

Scores support prioritization but do not replace written rationale.

## Promotion gates

Promote to `generalized` only after local assumptions are separated. Promote to `validated` only after representative checks can falsify the claim. Promote to `adoptable` only when validation, provenance, rights, risks, and integration ownership are resolved.

## Adoption decisions

- `adopt`: use substantially as-is after downstream integration.
- `adapt`: useful but requires explicit changes or host-specific wrapping.
- `research`: evidence, rights, or validation is insufficient.
- `reject`: unsafe, incorrect, redundant, non-portable, or unusable under available rights.

Include rejected candidates and reasons. Silent filtering makes the harvest impossible to audit.
