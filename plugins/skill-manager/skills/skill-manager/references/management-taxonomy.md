## Eight routes

### Inventory and discovery

List explicitly scoped skill roots, identities, metadata, hashes, provenance, validity, host discovery, and potential duplicate names.

### Install and update

Stage a new source or version, validate it, compare with the active version, activate through a supported mechanism, verify, and retain rollback.

### Enable, disable, and surface

Change whether an existing skill is offered or discoverable without confusing configuration state with file presence.

### Conflict resolution

Resolve duplicate names, overlapping descriptions, shadowed copies, namespaces, and ambiguous routing or precedence.

### Dependencies and supply chain

Review source provenance, scripts, packages, licenses, network behavior, secrets, integrity, update channels, and transitive dependencies.

### Governance and portfolio

Manage ownership, approval, policy, lifecycle status, versions, environments, audit evidence, and organization-wide rollout.

### Retirement and recovery

Quarantine, migrate consumers, disable, archive or remove, verify absence, and preserve recovery until completion.

### Dispatch and coordination

Route creation, diagnosis, optimization, installation, or multi-skill work to bounded specialists while retaining lifecycle authority.

## Classification rules

- Select the route controlling the highest-risk state transition.
- Inventory before every mutation.
- Resolve unsafe or broken skills through doctor before activation or optimization.
- Resolve identity conflicts before enablement.
- Review provenance and dependencies before installation.
- Retire only after consumers and rollback are known.

## Lifecycle and health

Lifecycle state answers where and whether a skill is used. Health answers whether it behaves correctly and safely. Structural validity answers whether its bundle parses. Do not substitute one for another.
